06 · About & founding

Built by an assembly, not an office.

On October 19, 2020, more than sixty individual co-founders — drawn from different Indigenous Nations across geographically diverse countries, convened as one General Assembly, before any formal Nation-to-Nation agreement yet existed between them — resolved to build this organization.

A council spanning all the continents our relatives call home.

By design, not yet by seat

UNIPC’s governance structure is fully designed and documented:

General Assembly

The body of members; where resolutions are made.

Executive Council

Carries the Assembly’s mandate between sessions.

Specialized councils

Culture, environment, community safety, and treasury.

Independent oversight bodies

Accountability that does not report to those it reviews.

At that founding Assembly, our co-founders also voted to designate who will carry each key role — held today as “[Role]-designate” — with the offices themselves remaining dormant until treaty ratification.

This is a deliberate choice: an institution with no operating seats has nothing for anyone to falsely claim to hold.

The principle that outranks every other line

We seek each nation’s membership so it may be part of a growing collective voice — never so that we may speak for it.

This is how our United Nations of Indigenous Peoples comes together: one voice, heard across every land.

A note on standing.

Co-Founding Member

A permanent historical designation, held for life by every participant in the original 2020 Assembly. This records history.

Active Co-Founding Member in Good Standing

A co-founder presently fulfilling their ongoing role and responsibilities within UNIPC. This reflects current, active contribution.

Both are honored; only the second carries present decision-making participation.

We stand in a long line. Indigenous nations were making treaties across this world before most modern states existed, and Indigenous diplomats have kept an unbroken presence in the world’s halls — from the first petitions in Geneva a century ago to the delegations that return there still. We did not begin this work. We are building it a permanent home.
