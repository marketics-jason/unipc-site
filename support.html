05 · Support the Recognition

Fair question: how does support actually work? Here’s the honest answer.

Reciprocity is the oldest Indigenous economic principle there is — and this page practices it. Nothing here is a donation to UNIPC directly. UNIPC holds no accounts to receive one.

How in-kind sponsorship works

A sponsor pays a named vendor directly for a specific, disclosed cost — including but not limited to office space, shipping logistics, or vendor gift cards. The invoice and payment are in the vendor’s name, never UNIPC’s. No cash, transfer, or credit of any kind reaches any co-founder or officer. A sponsor may also apply their own airline miles or hotel credits directly to travel costs.

Treaty-preparation materials require security paper and manufacturing for presentation booklets. A sponsor pays that supplier directly. The finished materials are delivered for UNIPC’s use. UNIPC never touches the funds.

Examples of in-kind support.

A few categories, to give a sense of the range — not a limit.

Technology & equipment

Laptops and devices for staff and community liaisons, satellite or field connectivity, solar chargers, office equipment.

Software & digital services

Cloud storage (with data-sovereignty options considered), website hosting, secure communication tools.

Space & logistics

Meeting or office space, storage for archives and materials, venue donations for gatherings and assemblies.

Transportation, travel & accommodations

Airline miles or travel vouchers, vehicle use for fieldwork, fuel or transport support, and accommodations — by far the single largest hard cost our co-founders carry for every step forward, and one of the most valuable forms of support available.

Communications & media

Printing, video production, photography, radio equipment and broadcast time for community stations.

Capacity building

Workshop facilitation in digital security, governance, language preservation, and financial literacy.

Community wellness

Food for assemblies and ceremonies, medical supplies for remote work, wellness support for those managing difficult content.

Educational & cultural materials

Books and recordings in Indigenous languages, oral-history equipment, cultural and craft materials.

And many other forms — if you have something specific in mind, ask us. Pro bono professional services (legal, accounting, grant-writing) are addressed separately, alongside our Internships & Volunteering program.

What your funding will — and won’t — achieve.

UNIPC always welcomes donors at every level — but we hold to some basics for all of them.

Your funding will…
  • ✓Advance real treaty-preparation and recognition work.
  • ✓Be tracked and disclosed above the transparency threshold.
  • ✓Be spent exactly as disclosed, with no hidden use.
Your funding will not…
  • ×Buy a vote — nations alone govern.
  • ×Buy influence over UNIPC’s direction.
  • ×Buy early access to information not otherwise public.

We spell out the “won’t” column because the donors we want most are the ones who read it and feel relieved, not deterred.

“If you want influence, we’re the wrong room. If you want to be early, you’re right on time.”
