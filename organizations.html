How we are met

UNIPC does not solicit or apply for institutional grants, IGO funding streams, or foundation calls-for-proposals — that positioning doesn’t reflect how we intend to be met. Where an Organization or institution wishes to extend support, in funds or in kind, it does so as a gift reflecting genuine interest and dedication to Indigenous Peoples’ advancement — welcomed, never pursued.

What we seek

UNIPC is being designed as the structural answer: an organization governed by Indigenous nations, able to receive support at institutional standards and direct it to where communities actually need it. Accreditation-grade fiduciary systems are in design, with any future regulated financial services pursued through proper authorization and announced only when licensed — never before.

Today

UNIPC does not hold funds, bank accounts, or accept monetary contributions of any kind — this is a deliberate design choice, not an oversight, made specifically so no one can claim to represent UNIPC while handling money on its behalf.

The one way presently available to support treaty-preparation work is in-kind sponsorship: paying a named vendor directly for a disclosed cost.

Accounting discipline, ahead of requirement

UNIPC has voluntarily committed to International Public Sector Accounting Standards from its earliest operating years — the same accounting discipline used by major intergovernmental organizations — well before any external requirement compels it.

Once UNIPC holds funds, that same discipline governs every contribution: unearmarked or declined, and no single funder positioned to define our direction.
