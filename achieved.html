07 · What we achieved

The record, so far, in plain terms.

We convened.

Our General Assembly met virtually, resolved, and appointed senior roles-designate, since 2020.

We approved.

A draft, with final revisions, of the Treaty of Tiohtiá:ke — a Multilateral Treaty of Friendship, Cooperation and Recognition — open for signature by States and International Organizations willing to stand publicly with Indigenous Peoples, the instrument through which they may extend recognition to UNIPC.

Nations joined.

Through a more prominent path together with our relatives: the Nation Treaty of Friendship and Unity, formalized as their own Nation-to-Nation membership agreement with UNIPC — with a companion Nations MOU available as a further, alternative route into full participation.

Indigenous Nations have made treaties among themselves and with others since time immemorial — a practice this Treaty does not create, but stands within, and a fundamental right of Nations to enter into treaty relations in their own standing, not one that flows from States alone.

We built.

The internal backbone: the UNIPC Constitutional Architecture and Framework for Recognition — a permanent Indigenous platform, with standards for consent and documentation, and protocols by which nations and states meet with respect.

We invite ongoing dialogue from Indigenous stakeholders to refine this living governance document, honoring the principle that true authority rests with the people.
